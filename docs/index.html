The problem

Photos lie. Guests can't judge what they're really ordering.

Static menu photos misrepresent portion size, layering, and presentation. Most "QR menus" today are just PDFs — no real innovation. Tourists struggle most with unfamiliar dishes. We surveyed real diners — the pain is near-universal.

have had a dish arrive looking different than expected — 74% said it bothered them.

How it works

Scan. Inspect in 3D. Place it on your table.

No download, no friction. The whole experience lives in the browser the guest already has open.

📲
STEP 01

Scan the QR code

A code on the table opens the menu instantly — categories, prices, and a live 3D thumbnail of every dish. Nothing to install.

🔄
STEP 02

Inspect in 3D

Tap any dish to rotate and zoom a photoreal model under realistic lighting. See the real layering and presentation, not a flattering photo.

🍔
STEP 03

Place it on your table

Tap "View on table" and the dish appears in AR at true size on the real table — Android WebXR or iOS Quick Look, straight from the browser.

The product

A complete WebAR menu platform — the restaurant does nothing technical.

We scan, build, host, and maintain. Restaurants self-manage their menu, prices, and branding from an admin panel, and get behavioral analytics most QR menus never provide.

🧊

Interactive 3D + AR

Rotate and zoom every dish, then place it on the real table. Android WebXR carousel + iOS ARKit Quick Look, no app install.

📊

Full analytics funnel

Session funnel, AR usage, per-item engagement, dwell time, and peak hours. Most QR menus hand the restaurant zero behavioral data.

🌐

Bilingual, app-free

Georgian and English with live switching, tuned for a tourism-heavy market. Loads in ~3 seconds from a QR scan.

🛠️

Self-service admin

Items, categories, prices, per-item AR scale, visibility, sort order, and thumbnails — edits go live instantly. No reprinting menus.

🎨

White-label theming

Day/night palettes, Google Fonts, and branding so each restaurant's menu matches its own identity.

⚡

Lightweight & fast

Static, CDN-served, offline-precached. 3D libraries load lazily so the menu never blocks on the AR engine.

The moat

Every dish we scan trains the AI that makes the next one nearly free.

Manual photogrammetry doesn't scale. The winner is whoever produces good 3D food models fastest and cheapest — so we're building that capability into a data flywheel we own.

01
Land restaurants

Every onboarding is a paid service — and a data-collection event.

02
Capture (photos → 3D) pairs

Each dish produces a training pair: source photos mapped to a finished, optimized model.

03
Fine-tune image-to-3D AI

At ~3,000 quality models — enough to cover most of the Georgian market — we fine-tune an open-source model on rented GPUs.

04
Production cost collapses

Cheaper, faster models let us undercut and out-scale anyone doing this by hand → more clients → more data.

models to cover the market
